# Distance and lighting: how much they hurt, and what helps (simulated on LFW)

## Summary (read this first)
Real LFW photos were degraded the way a bad bin would be (far away, dim, side-lit, warm lamp, over-exposed), then looked up with the
real pipeline. **These are simulated degradations of press photos, not your webcam**: they rank the fixes; the real camera confirms them.

1. **The recognition model barely cares about lighting** (92-98% on dim, dark, side-lit and warm photos) **and is only moderately hurt by distance**
   until faces get small. Over-exposure is its weakest case (84%).
2. **The quality gates, not the model, were rejecting people.** The first gates rejected 99% of small faces and 46% of warm-lamp photos that the model
   recognised 96-97% of the time. The blur measure mistook dim light for blur. Fix: two tiers of gates (strict for enrollment, lenient for identification)
   and a blur measure that standardises size and contrast. At the bin, warm-lamp photos went from 52% to 96% recognised and side light from 75% to 96%.
3. **Image-processing fixes do not help.** CLAHE and gamma correction do not improve the model and make dim/dark photos worse (dark: 92% down to 80-88%).
   Upscaling only helps by getting small faces past the size gate. Saving dark and small templates makes almost no difference in this test.
4. **Small faces are the real limit.** With sensor noise and JPEG the model alone recognises 88% at a measured 47 px, 73% at 38 px, 42% at 28 px, 6% at 21 px.
   The lenient limit is therefore 48 px (measured width of the detected face). Below that, "move closer" is more honest than a coin-flip match.
5. **No wrong names and no real false accepts anywhere.** "Strangers accepted: 1/400" in several rows is one LFW photo labelled Rosalyn Carter that
   also shows Jimmy Carter, who is enrolled: the match is correct, the label is the artefact (checked).

**Reading the size labels:** the condition names are *nominal*. The measured shortest side of the detected face is: clean 93 px, "~80 px" 66 px,
"~60 px" 47 px, "~45 px" 38 px, "~35 px" 28 px, "~25 px" 21 px. The 48 px identification limit therefore passes about 42% of the "~60 px" photos
(median 47 px) and none of the smaller ones.

## Supporting measurements
**Which old gate rejected what (300 photos, original raw blur measure):** side light: blur 18%; warm dim lamp: blur 44%; dark and dim: 1%; ~80 px: "too small" 99%.

**Blur measure under lighting (the same sharp photo):**

| Lighting | Old measure | Contrast-normalised |
|---|---|---|
| normal | 530 | 395 |
| dim x0.5 | 130 | 389 |
| dark x0.25 | 34 | 413 |
| side light | 160 | 304 |
| warm dim lamp | 120 | 394 |
| over-exposed x1.8 | 819 | 433 |

Real blur still reads low on the new measure (sigma 0/1/2/3/4/6: 395, 239, 106, 54, 33, 18). On 4,000 LFW faces its 1st percentile is 47 and the 5th is 71.

**Letting gated photos into identification (LFW, 150 people):** strict gates 751 probes, 99.2% recognised, 0 wrong; no gates 778 probes, 99.0% recognised, 0 wrong.
The 27 extra photos the gates used to reject were recognised 93% of the time with 0 wrong names. Strangers accepted did not change.

**Scoring rule (how a face is scored against a person's saved templates):**

| Rule | Genuine mean | Best stranger | Recognised with 0 strangers in |
|---|---|---|---|
| best template (used) | 68% | 56% | 91.6% |
| average template (centroid) | 71% | 62% | 87.4% |
| mean of best 2 | 65% | 56% | 86.0% |
| half best + half centroid | 70% | 59% | 90.3% |

The centroid raises the right person's score but raises strangers' more, so people separate worse. The best-template rule stays.

**Mirror-averaging (embed the face and its mirror image, average):** genuine mean 67.6% to 68.7% and recognised-with-0-strangers 91.1% to 92.7% when applied to both
saved templates and live frame (probe only: 91.8%). A real but small gain for double the processing, so it is not enabled.

**Why 90% similarity is not a usable bar:** only 1 of 1,031 same-person LFW photo pairs (0.1%) reaches 90%; the median is 62%. On a real webcam with webcam-made templates,
close frontal frames reach about 86-89%.

## Caveats
Simulated degradations, press photos, a 150-person gallery, and no spoof or lookalike tests. Fairness across groups was not measured.
